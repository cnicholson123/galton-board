How it works

Balls drain from the hopper and bounce down a triangular lattice under real gravity. Every peg strike is a coin flip: the ball hops one half-spacing left or right, landing on a peg in the next row. Bounciness and scatter change how the arcs look, never the odds.

After n rows a ball's bin k is simply how many times it went right, so bin counts follow Binomial(n, p) — the orange curve. With enough rows it approaches a normal curve with mean np and variance np(1−p): the central limit theorem, built out of pegs.

The χ² test pools sparse tail bins and reports a p-value. For a fair simulation p-values are uniformly spread, so expect an occasional "borderline" — that's randomness too.
